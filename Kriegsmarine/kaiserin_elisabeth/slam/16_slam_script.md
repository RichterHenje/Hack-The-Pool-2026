# 16 · Slam script (3 minutes, English, team voices)

Draft of 28.10. for the Projekt-Slam, Wed 14:00. Speakers are marked A, B, and C; the team
fills in the names in the room. Every number stands in a claim of `docs/06_slam.md`
(`uv run python -m scripts.slam_numbers --check`). Stage directions in parentheses and
`[DEMO n]` cues are not spoken. The live page is the team's `reise.html`; the fallback
screenshots are the owner's (day_1893-01-11.png, overview.png). Read it aloud once against a
timer before 14:00 and cut what runs over.

## Script

(slide 1: the day card of 11 January 1893)

Speaker A: Good afternoon. On 11 January 1893 the cruiser SMS Kaiserin Elisabeth lay off Ceylon, and the heir to the throne went hunting at Kalawewa. His diary has a heading for that day, on scan 75. [DEMO 1: reise.html at 1893-01-11, the day card] This page shows one day of a voyage of 309 diary days, from 14 December 1892 to 18 October 1893. The interface is German; we name what you see. Our question for two days was short. The ship's archive and Kulturpool describe the same voyage. Where do they meet?

(slide 2: the gap, one number)

Speaker B: We placed 70 of the 90 stations the ship's archive names, from 1892 to 1914, on a map. For each station we asked Kulturpool what it holds for that place in the years of the voyage. 24 stations have such a record. Two records name the ship. The museums that hold the voyage, Weltmuseum Wien, KHM, the Heeresgeschichtliches Museum, and the Staatsarchiv, do not appear as data providers in the portal today. One finding surprised us. The Weltmuseum photographs of the voyage have been in Europeana since 2019, delivered through Kulturpool as aggregator. Search the portal for Tandur-Camp today, and you find nothing. The delivery exists; the portal does not show it. That is a question for Kulturpool, not for the museum.

(slide 3: against the grain)

Speaker C: We also read the diary against the grain. The route is the official story. Beside it we placed what the diary says day by day: the hunting, the animals killed and collected, the people in the photographs. [DEMO 2: the hunt layer on the day card] The sentences on this layer come from automatic text recognition, and nobody has checked them against the scans yet. We say so on every card. 131 of the 134 Europeana photographs of the voyage carry a Creative Commons licence, and 58 of them now sit on our map with their rights label. [DEMO 3: one photograph with its licence]

(slide 4: what it means for Kulturpool)

Speaker A: The archive stays as it is. The links are a separate layer beside it, each with its source, and that layer grows with every provider who connects. Nothing in the archive is rewritten. Five minutes on the page bring up what nobody searched for: a hunt, a photograph, a newspaper report of the same week.

(slide 5: the ask)

Speaker B: We ask three things. Tell us which Kulturpool records belong to this voyage. Connect the museums that hold it. Help us check the hunting sentences against the scans, one day at a time. Thank you.

## Ask

1. Kulturpool and ACDH: which records in the portal belong to this voyage, and why does a delivery of 2019 not surface in today's portal?
2. The museums that hold the voyage (Weltmuseum Wien, KHM, Heeresgeschichtliches Museum, Staatsarchiv): connect, or tell us where the records are.
3. Everyone in the room: 45 minutes with a scan and a sheet to check hunting sentences, so that machine output becomes evidence.
